# Channel Voice Guide

This is the established tone for the hy.dev TikTok channel. Read this fully before writing any scripts — consistency across projects matters more than any single video being clever.

## Channel identity

**Positioning:** An indie dev building real products for the SEA market, telling true engineering stories — not generic tutorials.
**Format:** Storytelling/concept explainer. No live coding, no screen recording of an IDE.
**Language:** English.
**Audience:** Developers and indie-hacker-adjacent people globally, with a specific edge for SEA-aware viewers.
**Goal:** Build a relevant audience that converts into waitlist signups / early users — not raw view count, not TikTok Creator Fund revenue.

## Why this voice, not a generic "tech tiktok" voice

Most coding content on TikTok is either (a) live-coding/tutorial, or (b) generic "explain X in 60 seconds" with no real product behind it (Fireship-style, excellent but not ownable by an indie dev). This channel's unfair advantage is that every video is a true story from a product actually being built, with real planning docs behind it. The voice should make that obvious — specific numbers, specific trade-offs, specific market context — never generic "best practices" advice.

## Sentence-level rules

- Contractions always ("it's" not "it is", "I'm" not "I am")
- Sentences short enough to say in one breath. If a sentence needs a comma-pause to read aloud comfortably, split it.
- No buzzwords: avoid "game-changer," "revolutionary," "10x," "disrupting," "next-level," "seamless" unless directly quoting something. These read as filler, not insight.
- No fake humility ("just a simple app") and no overselling ("this will change how you build apps"). State what it does and why, plainly.
- First person, singular ("I built," "I chose") — this is one person's real build, not a team or brand voice.
- Numbers stated plainly, not dramatized. "$15 a month at 100 users" not "an insanely cheap $15."

## Structural rules (apply to every script)

- **Hook (0–3s):** A claim or a question. Never "Hey guys," "So today I want to talk about," or any throat-clearing. The first sentence must work with zero context — assume the viewer has never seen this channel before.
- **Setup (3–15s):** Name the product once. One sentence of what it does. Don't explain the whole product — just enough for the next part to make sense.
- **Turn/Insight (15–45s):** This is the actual content. One idea per video — resist the urge to pack in two decisions or two numbers. If there's a second idea worth covering, that's a separate video.
- **Payoff/CTA (45–60s):** A takeaway sentence, then optionally a soft mention of the product (waitlist, follow-along framing). Never a hard sell. Never stack two products' CTAs in one script.

## What "one product per video" means in practice

Even if two projects share a technical decision (e.g. both use SQLDelight), do not combine them into one video. Pick whichever project's docs make the stronger, more specific case, and use that one. A second video about the other project's use of the same tech is fine later, but each script stays single-product.

## Pillar-specific voice notes

**The Decision:** This is the most "technical credibility" pillar. It's fine to use a real technical term (WebSocket, mTLS, AES-GCM) without over-explaining it — the audience is developer-adjacent. But always tie the term back to a plain-language reason a non-expert would still understand (e.g. "your screen and clipboard would pass through someone else's server" beats "it reduces attack surface").

**Building for SEA:** This pillar should feel like local insight a Western dev wouldn't have, not a geography lesson. Don't explain what Vietnam or SEA is — assume the viewer either knows or doesn't need to. Focus entirely on the specific product gap or behavior difference.

**Indie Math:** Numbers are the hook here, not the moral. Lead with the number, not with "let me tell you about costs." Real numbers from COST.md only — never round to make a cleaner story, never omit a cost category to make the number look better.

## Things this channel never does

- Never claims a feature is unique/first if the docs don't say so
- Never disparages a named competitor by name (general "most apps do X" framing is fine, naming and bashing a specific competitor is not)
- Never uses growth-hacking language about the content itself ("this video will blow up")
- Never ends on a cliffhanger that isn't paid off ("part 2 coming" only if there genuinely is a planned part 2)
